## Primary-document side-by-side comparison

**1936 FHA Underwriting Manual**, revision April 1, 1936, Part II, Rating of Location, paragraph **284(3)(g)**: recommends deed restrictions excluding occupancy by races other than the one intended for the property. Primary scanned edition and imperfect searchable transcription: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/underwriting-manual-6279/underwriting-manual-602318/fulltext (search paragraph 284(3)(g)).

**1938 FHA Underwriting Manual**, revised through February 1938, Part II, Rating of Location, paragraph **980(3)(g)**: includes the same race-restrictive occupancy provision among suggested restrictive covenants. Primary scanned edition and searchable transcription: https://fraser.stlouisfed.org/title/underwriting-manual-6279/underwriting-manual-602319/fulltext (search paragraph 980(3)(g)).

**Comparison:** The discriminatory occupancy restriction occurs in **both** editions, with different section numbering. Its presence by April 1936 rules out characterizing it as newly introduced in the February 1938 edition. The source establishes FHA's published underwriting guidance; it does **not** establish the number of approvals/denials caused by this passage, whether each office applied it uniformly, or national causal outcomes. ## Historical quantitative indicator reproduced

Source: Price Fishback, Jonathan Rose, Kenneth Snowden, Thomas Storrs, *New Evidence on Redlining by Federal Housing Programs in the 1930s*, Federal Reserve Bank of Chicago working paper 2022-01, Table 2, **printed page 38 / PDF page index 38**:

Measure: **percent distribution of each loan program's recorded loans by later HOLC neighborhood grade**. Percentages represent shares of FHA-insured loans or HOLC loans within each city, NOT approval or denial rates. HOLC map classifications were often assigned AFTER the earliest loans, therefore grade overlays alone DO NOT establish map-driven exclusion.

Note: Table 2 values are reproduced as published with rounding, so a row may not sum to exactly 100. To make the empirical context inspectable, the researchers report 3,540 FHA-insured loans and 10,145 HOLC loans in Baltimore, 556 FHA and 982 HOLC in Peoria, and 238 FHA and 624 HOLC in Greensboro (paper printed pp. 12-13). These are three *sample cities*, not national counts; not every loan can be geolocated or census-matched. The researchers explicitly report differences in sampling coverage.

**Illustrative descriptive contrast:** In Baltimore, FHA-insured loans in grade-D neighborhoods accounted for **1.6%** of the city's FHA sample, whereas HOLC loans in grade-D neighborhoods accounted for **23.2%** of the city's HOLC sample. This is a comparison of **loan-distribution shares within different lending programs**, not a racial lending denial rate nor a causal estimate.

## Additional limits

- HOLC was a separate institution and financed an existing-homeowner refinancing program, while FHA insured qualifying mortgages through lenders. The policies and populations served differ.
- FHA borrower records in this study span 1935–1940; HOLC records span 1933–1936. The 1940 population and homeowner distributions are contemporaneous comparator data, not 1934 baseline effects.
- A direct comparison of geographic shares does not adjust for program eligibility, preexisting demographics, lender participation, neighborhood-level demand, housing stock, or other selection processes.
- Paper is an author analysis; POWER has reproduced published table cells and locators, but has **not independently rerun underlying mortgage-record reconstruction or regression specifications**.
- The 1936 versus 1938 manual comparison demonstrates explicit wording in two editions, not how a given insurer scored an individual application.
