## Quantitative research register

DOI 10.1016/j.jue.2022.103462. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0094119022000390
- **R-11** Earlier openly indexed working paper and data appendix: NBER Working Paper 29244 (September 2021), https://www.nber.org/papers/w29244

### Empirical scope to preserve
The authors report a roughly 16,000-loan dataset covering **Baltimore City, Maryland; Peoria, Illinois; and Greensboro, North Carolina**, with HOLC loans dated 1933–1936 and FHA insurance dated 1935–1940. The research draws on local recorded mortgages and Census-linked borrower details.

### Attributed research findings, NOT independently replicated
The authors report that FHA mortgage insurance largely excluded low-income central urban areas and Black mortgage borrowers in their sample, whereas HOLC refinancing was more geographically and demographically distributed. The authors also find limited evidence that later HOLC security maps directed FHA's early allocation patterns. These are **findings of the cited authors**, not independent POWER measurements or an all-United-States census.

### Causal caution
HOLC map causal-impact analyses investigate a different treatment from FHA underwriting rules. Do not infer that finding a pre-map FHA exclusion pattern disproves every potential downstream map effect. Separate outcome measures, units, geographical boundaries, pre-trends, and identification assumptions.
