## Critical distinction
The Federal Housing Administration (FHA) and Home Owners' Loan Corporation (HOLC) were separate agencies with different mandates and loan patterns. Do not equate HOLC security maps with FHA underwriting decisions, or attribute every observed neighborhood effect to one policy.

## Candidate findings: NOT approved for verified publication
1. **Underwriting policy:** Federal Reserve History (FHA-R01) reports that the 1938 FHA Underwriting Manual supported racially exclusionary neighborhood underwriting and restrictive covenants. This is a sourced secondary description; the precise contemporaneous edition and manual sections must be checked before direct quote or primary-document classification.
2. **Lending geography:** Fishback et al. (FHA-R02) find that FHA mortgage-insurance activity largely excluded low-income core urban neighborhoods and Black borrowers, whereas HOLC lending reached more of these areas. Record this as an attributed empirical finding, not as a universal count or comprehensive administrative rule.
3. **Timing/mechanism:** Fishback et al. report exclusionary FHA patterns predating HOLC maps and limited evidence that HOLC maps drove FHA's geographic allocations. This does not preclude HOLC map effects via other channels.
4. **Long-run HOLC effects:** Aaronson et al. (FHA-R04) estimate persistent neighborhood differences following HOLC map grading. Keep their identification strategy, studied boundaries, uncertainty, and time periods attached to any future outcome series.

## Proposed audit decomposition
- **Statutory authority:** National Housing Act text (1934).
- **Administration:** FHA underwriting manuals, internal instructions, staffing and underwriting decisions over time.
- **Geographic practice:** Loan-level or tract/block-level FHA insurance activity compared with HOLC lending, where documented.
- **Independent HOLC mechanism:** Map production, audience, dissemination and links to credit access.
- **Distributional evidence:** Documented borrower/area access, accounting for changing racial categories and boundaries.
- **Outcomes and causation:** Contemporaneous lending and later neighborhood indicators, separating associations and identification-based estimates.
- **Legal changes:** Subsequent laws and enforcement, separately sourced and time-indexed.

## Methodological safeguards
The difference between **statutory authorization**, **actual implementation**, **observed statistical association**, and **causal attribution** is mandatory. HOLC maps and FHA insurance underwriting are not interchangeable exposures. Secondary studies may support attributed empirical claims, but they do not transform those findings into independent POWER verification without reproducibility review. Conflicting or methodologically distinct studies should be linked side-by-side rather than collapsed into a single verdict.
