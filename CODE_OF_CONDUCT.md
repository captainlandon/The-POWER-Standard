# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

The POWER Standard is a public-interest civic technology project. We want participation in the project to be constructive, rigorous, accessible, and welcoming to people with different backgrounds, disciplines, political perspectives, and levels of technical experience.

Contributors are expected to engage one another in ways that support the project's nonpartisan, evidence-disciplined mission.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that supports this community include:

- treating other contributors with respect and good faith;
- criticizing ideas, evidence, methods, and code without attacking people;
- explaining technical, civic, legal, or policy concepts when clarification is useful;
- distinguishing evidence from interpretation and uncertainty;
- being transparent about relevant conflicts of interest;
- welcoming correction when sources, assumptions, or implementations are wrong;
- designing for accessibility and people with different levels of civic literacy;
- avoiding partisan advocacy inside project contributions unless a political position itself is the object of sourced analysis;
- respecting privacy, safety, and the dignity of people represented in civic data.

## Unacceptable behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, stalking, or discriminatory abuse;
- personal attacks or sustained bad-faith disruption;
- doxxing or publishing private information without permission;
- deliberately falsifying, misrepresenting, or fabricating civic data or sources;
- using the project to campaign for or against candidates, parties, or protected political factions;
- presenting speculation as verified fact;
- knowingly introducing inaccessible or deceptive civic interaction patterns;
- retaliation against people who raise good-faith concerns.

## Evidence and disagreement

Disagreement is expected in a project dealing with government, public policy, evidence, and democratic accountability. Contributors should make disagreements inspectable: cite sources, state assumptions, identify uncertainty, and explain methodological differences.

Nonpartisanship does not require false balance. Claims with stronger evidence may appropriately receive stronger evidentiary treatment, provided the reasoning and sources are transparent and contestable.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in repository discussions, issues, pull requests, project meetings, community spaces, and other activities conducted on behalf of The POWER Standard.

## Reporting and enforcement

If you experience or observe conduct that violates this policy, report it privately to the project maintainer through an appropriate project contact channel. Reports should include enough information to understand and review the concern while avoiding unnecessary disclosure of sensitive personal information.

Project maintainers may remove content, close or lock discussions, reject contributions, restrict participation, or take other proportionate action when necessary to protect the project and its community.

Enforcement decisions should be documented internally and applied as consistently as reasonably possible.

## Civic responsibility

Because POWER is intended to help people understand public authority, evidence, accountability, and participation, contributors have an additional responsibility to avoid designs or data practices that create false certainty, obscure provenance, exaggerate public influence, or misrepresent institutional responsibility.

The standard we apply to public institutions should also apply to the project itself: methodology, corrections, governance decisions, and uncertainty should remain open to scrutiny.
