Platform architecture

A public instrument for the life of a plan.

POWER begins before an election, remains useful after it, and preserves the record long enough for learning. This is the product architecture, not a claim that every module is already live.

Current status

The public website is an explanatory prototype. The Plan Builder and thin Mandate Ledger are the proposed initial pilot. The complete ecosystem below is the long-range product vision.

The core political instrument

The Plan Builder is the interface used by candidates, campaigns, elected officials, public offices, or POWER researchers compiling a record from public statements. It turns a promise into a plan that can be understood before an election and revisited afterward.

Candidate participation without a veto

POWER should invite candidates and offices to submit or verify their own plans. If they decline, trained researchers may compile a clearly labeled record from speeches, websites, filings, interviews, and other public sources. The interface must distinguish:

  • Candidate-submitted text
  • Candidate-verified compilation
  • POWER-compiled public record
  • Disputed interpretation or unanswered request

From campaign to durable public record

1. Define

A problem, office, authority map, evidence base, and plan record are established.

2. Compare

The public sees materially comparable plans without flattening meaningful differences.

3. Preserve

The version presented to the public is timestamped and retained. Later revisions do not overwrite it.

4. Follow

Budgets, legislation, agency actions, implementation events, explanations, and barriers are linked.

5. Evaluate and learn

Outcomes and evidence are assessed with uncertainty, causation limits, and historical context intact.

Forecasts and behavioral signals

A Forecast and Expectation Layer may show public or market expectations, but it must never be confused with legal authority, feasibility, action, or outcome. A later Panoptica signal layer may flag patterns associated with practices such as monopoly, gerrymandering, capture, conflicts of interest, or suspicious resource flows. Signals would prompt investigation and explanation, not declare illegality or guilt.
