Start with the rules

Government has powers and limits.

The Constitution establishes the federal system. Learn who has authority before deciding who should act. Federal, state, local, tribal and DC institutions have different responsibilities.

Make it practical. Ask: which level of government can make this decision?

Who does what?

Laws, administration and courts.

Congress makes federal laws. The executive branch carries them out. Courts interpret and apply law in cases. Power is shared and constrained; one office cannot deliver every promise alone.

Make it practical. Ask: does this require a law, an agency action, or a legal process?

How decisions happen

Follow the stage, not just the headline.

A proposal, a passed law, an agency action and a completed project are different records. Identify the decision owner, dependencies and what remains open before choosing how to participate.

Make it practical. A letter can ask an office to act or explain. It does not itself change a law or guarantee a response.

04 / 08

Follow public money

A budget is the beginning of the trail.

Fiscal policy concerns government taxation and spending. In the federal budget process the President proposes a budget and Congress considers funding legislation. Separate authorization to spend, legal obligations, payments and delivered results.

Make it practical. Ask: is this a proposed amount, approved funding, or money actually paid?

05 / 08

Government and the economy

Fiscal and monetary policy are different.

Government tax and spending choices are fiscal policy. The Federal Reserve conducts monetary policy to influence financial conditions in pursuit of maximum employment and stable prices. A local housing agency does not set Federal Reserve interest rates.

Make it practical. Ask: which institution controls the policy tool being discussed?

From policy to everyday life

Housing is a useful place to begin.

A housing goal, housing finance, project selection and a finished affordable home are distinct stages. A higher unit count does not by itself prove that an existing renter can afford their home.

Make it practical. Ask: who benefited, over what period, and what is still unknown?

Check what happened

Keep the evidence and the unknowns.

Read an original document, check its date and scope, and distinguish what an institution reports from what other evidence corroborates. Preserve corrections and responses. Uncertainty should remain visible.

Make it practical. Ask: what does this source establish, and what does it not establish?

Investigate a real example • Washington, DC

Did the housing promise become housing?

Follow three original records. They cover different periods and stages; they do not establish a direct link from this budget to all citywide production.

  1. The goal: DC describes a target of 36,000 new homes by 2025, including 12,000 affordable, and 6,000 preserved affordable homes.
  2. The resource: the FY2022 emergency budget act lists $250 million for HPTF under Enterprise and Other (7). That is an appropriation, not a verified payment total.
  3. The action: DHCD reported selecting 69 preservation projects on February 20, 2025. Selection does not establish completed preservation.

Still unknown in this short trail: matched project disbursements, completed preservation, and residents’ resulting housing costs. Ask for these records before drawing a conclusion.

Your next step

Prepare a call or letter.

Choose the level, verify the responsible office, and put your own request into words. Your draft stays in this page’s memory until you leave or reload; POWER does not send or save it.

Open the official contact directory ↗

Verify the office, jurisdiction, constituency rules and current contact information on its official website. ZIP codes alone may not identify a congressional district. DC’s local government and federal representation require different routes.

Your draft

To call, use the number on the verified official page. To write, paste your draft into that office’s form or use its listed postal address. No call or message has been sent by POWER.

Close the loop

Keep your own note of when you contacted the office, any acknowledgement, the response, and what changed. Contact made, response received, and policy changed are three different outcomes.
